## 1. World and system intent

Everbound is an original high-fantasy, isekai/JRPG-inspired Adventurers Guild world with a shared persistent world state. Players may join different sessions, collaborate with different adventurers and GMs, and preserve the effects of their actions between adventures. Guild rank runs **F through SS**, but rank expresses reputation and accomplishments rather than automatically determining character level or class power.

Six **fixed starting classes** are available at registration. Players **cannot invent or select a custom class**. Classes should be equally valuable across combat, exploration, investigation, social scenes, and travel. Character differentiation comes from generic skills, advanced specialization skills, eventual racial traits, and in-world choices.

**Important distinction:** The class **names and six-class roster are official**. Skill descriptions and structure below form the working **v1.0 design draft**. Dice systems, exact numbers, resource economies, skills' action types, and some advanced restrictions remain subject to testing.

### Class identities

- **Vanguard:** Holds ground, breaks enemy formations, and shields companions through discipline and resilience.
- **Wildstrider:** Uses distance, movement, terrain knowledge, and perception to discover routes and exploit environmental advantages. Works in cities, ruins, or wilds.
- **Shadowblade:** Uses timing, stealth, misdirection, and precision to expose weaknesses and create openings; not defined by a criminal background.
- **Luminary:** Channels restorative light and protective energy. Practice need not be bound to any one deity or religious order.
- **Arcanist:** Manipulates magical forces to reshape battlefields and investigate mysterious artifacts and mechanisms.
- **Resonant:** Channels vibration, rhythm, sound, and emotional connection; instruments are optional, not mandatory.

## 4. Generic skill-tree rules

Every class has **three universal development paths**, each with **four progressive skills** (**12 trainable skills per class**, **72 in total**). A player can invest in any combination of their class's three paths. These paths are **not** specializations.

**Proposed cost and progression:**

- Gain **Skill Points (SP)** through character progression; earning rate is **not finalized**.
- Each generic skill costs **1 SP** to learn.
- Unlocks run **Tier I → Tier II → Tier III → Tier IV** within each path.
- Unlock the directly preceding skill in that path before purchasing a higher tier.
- Meet the cumulative **class-tree SP investment** for the tier:

| Generic tier | Class-tree SP already spent | Purpose |
|---|---:|---|
| I — Foundation | 0 | Establish playstyle |
| II — Development | 2 | Strengthen abilities |
| III — Expertise | 5 | Develop advanced tactics |
| IV — Mastery | 8 | Powerful universal techniques |

**Interpretation:** The SP thresholds are points already invested *in the character's generic class tree*, not necessarily total career SP. Actual character-level thresholds and reward cadence remain open. Generic skill trees are available to **every race** that can choose the corresponding class.

## 6. Advanced specialization system

A specialization is an advanced discipline of an existing class; it is **not** a new starting class. Everbound has **three specializations per class**, each with:

1. **One automatic Discipline Trait** (free upon unlocking that specialization).
2. **Four trainable specialization skills**, Tiers I–IV, unlocked sequentially for **1 SP each**.

That is **18 specializations**, **18 Discipline Traits**, and **72 trainable advanced skills**.

### Proposed unlock and training flow

1. Register as one of the six official starting classes.
2. Invest **at least 5 SP in the generic class tree**.
3. Complete a suitable **Guild specialization trial**, mentor training, or quest achievement.
4. Select **one primary active specialization** and receive its Discipline Trait.
5. Spend SP to learn that specialization's Tier I, then II, III, and IV skills in order.
6. Continue acquiring generic class skills independently.

**Playtest restriction:** One **active specialization** at a time. Retraining/switching occurs **between adventures** through a Guild trainer, not in the middle of an encounter or quest. The precise cost and handling of previously learned inactive-specialization skills are not yet finalized. Cross-specialization training may be an advanced future option.

**Tier IV abilities:** Usually useable **once per encounter** or under a similarly restrictive resource cost; exact action/resource types will be assigned after the core mechanics are finalized.

## 7. Specialization trees — all 18

### 7.1 Vanguard specializations

**Guardian — The Last Wall:** A defensive protector who gives up mobility and offensive opportunities to prevent allies from falling. Built around interception and sacrifice.

**Berserker — The Untamed Fury:** An aggressive melee fighter who turns pain and fury into momentum, deliberately risking personal safety for overwhelming pressure.

**Warlord — The Battlefield Commander:** A nonmagical tactical leader who coordinates willing allies, maneuvers, and timing without controlling other players' decisions.

| Level | **Guardian** | **Berserker** | **Warlord** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Trait** | **Protector's Oath** — Gain a small defensive advantage while deliberately guarding a nearby ally. | **Fierce Resolve** — Resist fear and hesitation more effectively while wounded. | **Battle Reader** — Identify enemy formations and their most obvious tactical weaknesses. |
| **Tier I** | **Covering Presence** — Offer partial protection against ranged attacks to an adjacent ally while holding position. | **Reckless Surge** — Empower a melee strike, but leave your defenses temporarily exposed. | **Order to Advance** — Allow a willing ally to reposition a short distance when you successfully engage an enemy. |
| **Tier II** | **Bastion Step** — When using Stand Before Me, guide the protected ally out of immediate danger. | **Bloodied Drive** — Gain a burst of movement toward a threat when badly injured. | **Focused Assault** — Improve a nearby ally's attack against an enemy you have marked. |

**Design boundaries:** Guardian prioritizes damage interception; Berserker has genuine vulnerability costs; Warlord grants opportunities to *willing* allies and should not create free, unlimited full actions.

A routine combat defeat should incapacitate/withdraw the companion pending recovery, not permanently remove it.

**World rules:** Luminary magic is not necessarily religious. Spirit communication can reveal clues but spirits may be unwilling, unable to remember, or limited in what they know. Major curses and world events should require quests rather than being trivialized by one purification skill.

### 7.5 Arcanist specializations

**Elementalist — The Primal Weaver:** A master of flexible elemental magic and the interactions between elements and surroundings.

**Runecaster — The Architect of Sigils:** A magical engineer who prepares, links, and triggers inscriptions to protect areas or set traps.

**Spellbinder — The Arcane Sovereign:** A counter-mage who reads magical structures and weakens or modifies enemy spells.

| Level | **Elementalist** | **Runecaster** | **Spellbinder** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Trait** | **Elemental Attunement** — Select a preferred element during preparation, gaining enhanced understanding and minor utility involving that element. | **Runic Kit** — Prepare and carry a limited supply of magical inscription materials for specialized rune abilities. | **Pattern Reader** — Improve your ability to recognize the structure and likely purpose of spells you witness. |

| **Tier II** | **Adaptive Ward** — Gain temporary resistance against an elemental effect you have identified. | **Trigger Rune** — Place a temporary rune that releases a disruptive magical effect when triggered. | **Seal of Stillness** — Temporarily weaken a magical creature's active enhancement or a maintained spell effect. |

| **Tier IV** | **Elemental Convergence** — Combine two elemental effects into a powerful, controlled magical event. | **Master Matrix** — Establish an interconnected group of runes that controls and protects a limited battlefield area. | **Arcane Dominion** — Briefly impose a localized magical restriction that weakens enemy spellcasting within its area. |

**Elementalist's flexible affinities:**

| Element | Possible tactical identity |
|---|---|
| Fire | Burning, ignition |
| Frost | Slowing, freezing |
| Lightning | Shocks, disruption |
| Wind | Movement, pushing |

Instead of creating four separate elemental trees immediately, Elementalists use skills that vary according to element. More advanced elemental mastery can be added later. **Runecasters** require preparation and should have a limited number of active runes. **Spellbinders** must not automatically negate every spell or boss ability.

### 7.6 Resonant specializations

**Warcaller — The Voice of Victory:** A combat coordinator whose signals, rhythms, and commands improve group maneuvering.

**Echo Sage — The Keeper of Reverberations:** A sound investigator and illusionist who can interpret echoes and misdirect through resonance.

**Harmonist — The Heart of the Company:** A cooperative support specialist who creates lasting magical links between allies and reinforces resilience.

| Level | **Warcaller** | **Echo Sage** | **Harmonist** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Trait** | **Commanding Cadence** — Allies find your clear tactical signals easier to follow amid battle noise and confusion. | **Acoustic Awareness** — Identify unusual reverberations, sound distortions, and concealed spaces more effectively. | **Matching Pulse** — Establish a gentle magical connection that improves coordination with one willing ally. |

**World rules:** Resonants can channel power via voice, movement, instruments, or other expressive forms. Echo Sage sound remnants are **imperfect clues**, not guaranteed complete recordings. A Resonant's buffs should improve allied choices without overriding player agency.

## 8. Rules, party balance, and GM consistency

The game is intended to operate with multiple GMs across a shared persistent world. Ability terminology and encounter limits need standardized definitions.

### 8.1 Proposed rules for first playtests

| Mechanic | Working rule |
|---|---|
| Starting class | Select **exactly one** of six official classes; no player-created classes. |
| Class foundation | Receive the class's **four** starter abilities automatically. |
| Universal skill price | **1 SP** per generic skill. |
| Generic paths | All three generic paths are available to every character of that class. |
| Generic prerequisites | Previous tier in the same path and cumulative generic class SP threshold. |
| Specialization entry | **5 generic class-tree SP**, plus Guild specialization trial or equivalent training. |
| Primary specialization | One active specialization during the first playtest. |
| Specialization trait | Automatically acquired on activating/unlocking the specialization. |
| Specialization skill price | **1 SP** per tier, purchased in order I → IV. |
| Tier IV / mastery | Usually once per encounter, pending final resource mechanics. |
| Retraining | Between adventures through a Guild trainer; exact price pending. |
| Guild rank | **F–SS**, based on Guild reputation and accomplishments; separate from class skill progression. |
| Race restrictions | **No race restrictions** on generic class skills. Future race skills remain separate and compatible. |
| Duplicate benefits | Normally do **not stack** unless the rule explicitly says they do. |
| Encounter boundaries | Determined by narrative combat state, not GM handoffs or changing tables. |

### 8.2 Ability classification vocabulary

Record one or more of the following classifications in the character website and GM reference:

- **Passive:** An always-available conditional effect.
- **Action:** Uses the adventurer's normal action or an equivalent turn opportunity.
- **Reaction:** Responds to an event, consuming the standard reaction opportunity.
- **Field:** Used outside combat in travel, investigation, social scenes, or preparation; may still require an appropriate check.
- **Mastery:** A high-tier effect with a strict encounter or resource limit.

> **Not yet assigned:** Individual skill action types, detailed durations, ranges, area dimensions, resource costs, exact bonuses, healing amounts, attack dice, damage, and conditions. These are deliberately left qualitative until the core engine is defined.

### 8.3 Shared-table balance safeguards

1. **No infinite reaction chains.** All reaction-style effects use a shared defined reaction limit, even if several skills could trigger.
2. **No unrestricted extra turns.** Ally repositioning and coordinated attacks cannot create infinite full actions or recursive turn-granting combos.
3. **No automatic campaign bypass.** Stealth, teleport-like abilities, social influence, spirit conversations, rune tools, and purification must have obstacles and consequences.
4. **No arbitrary instant kills.** Precision attacks can be exceptionally strong without automatically destroying a boss or major foe.
5. **No unrestricted summons.** Companion creatures do not add an unconstrained combat turn.
6. **No boundless preparation stacks.** Trap/rune quantities, simultaneous magical effects, and duration limits must be capped.
7. **No mandatory class combinations.** Groups without a designated healer, defender, or spellcaster must have workable quest options.
8. **No free resource refresh on table changes.** Quest/encounter state follows the character across GM handoffs.
9. **Respect other players' agency.** Command, charm-like effects, coordinated abilities, and social buffs cannot force willing allies or NPCs to behave outside the rule's stated reach.
10. **Preserve world consequences.** Skills may help identify and resolve world problems but do not erase major story arcs for free.

### 8.4 Cooperation and specialization examples

**Example A — Two Vanguards:**

- A Bulwark + Guardian character protects companions, absorbs danger, and defends objectives.
- A Breaker + Berserker character pushes enemies, attacks aggressively, and accepts exposure for more damage.

**Example B — Different Wildstriders:**

- A Sharpshot + Deadeye character invests in prepared precision attacks.
- A Wayfinding + Trailblazer character helps parties survive difficult journeys and find escape routes.
- A Fieldcraft + Beastwarden character supports reconnaissance and coordinated creature tactics.

**Example C — Two Arcanists:**

- A Runecraft + Runecaster character prepares defensive traps and magical mechanisms.
- A Shaping + Spellbinder character interferes with enemy magic and adapts battlefield spells.

## 10. Future racial skills and open decisions

### 10.1 Race skills — next design phase

Race progression has **not** been developed in this compendium. It should be a third progression layer, accessible independently of the universal class tree:

```
Character Registration
  ├─ Choose one race
  └─ Choose one of six fixed classes
            │
            └─ Four free starter abilities
                 │
                 ├─ Generic class tree: 3 paths × 4 tiers
                 │       └─ Specialization trial
                 │            └─ Active specialization: 1 trait + 4 tiers
                 │
                 └─ Racial skill tree (future design)
```

**Race design principles:**

- Racial abilities should complement a class, not make one class-race pairing universally optimal.
- Every race should retain meaningful strengths regardless of class.
- Race selection should not lock generic class skills.
- Avoid creating a huge custom skill catalogue for every race × class × specialization combination at the outset.
- Later race-specialization interactions may add situational synergies without replacing the base system.
- Keep racial appearance and class playstyle independent; gender and race need not prescribe equipment, personality, or combat role.

### 10.2 Open mechanical decisions (not yet approved)

- What dice or check resolution system does Everbound use?
- How many skill points are earned and at which character milestones?
- How do attack rolls, damage, healing, defense, and conditions scale?
- What is the action economy (main action, movement, reactions, off-turn support)?
- What are the exact range, duration, area, resource, and usage limits on every ability?
- How are encounter boundaries defined during multi-GM and drop-in play?
- What is the cost and persistence behavior of specialization retraining?
- Are all specialization skills permanently learned but only one specialization active, or does retraining refund/reassign skills?
- When, if ever, can a veteran adventurer train across two specializations?
- What are the companion command, injury, recovery, and progression rules for Beastwarden?
- How many simultaneous runes and maintained spells are allowed?
- How will environmental elemental interactions be standardized for GMs?
- How will racial progression unlock relative to class progression?
- What automated checks will the character portal enforce when buying a skill?

**Recommended sequencing:** Finalize core dice/action/resource rules → translate all qualitative abilities into testable values → playtest the six generic trees and 18 specializations → create the racial skill trees → test race/class/specialization interactions → publish revised balance edition.

## 11. Lineage × Class Specialization Affinities

**Version:** 1.1 (proposed integration layer; recommendations removed)  
**Updated:** November 23, 2026  
**Status:** Thematic lineage–specialization pairings for playtesting; **not** recommendations or locked mechanical unlocks  
**Source roster:** Approved *Everbound Racial Lineage Compendium v1.0*  
**Companion reference:** *Everbound Class & Specialization Compendium v1.0*

> **Scope:** 12 base races × 5 approved lineages × 6 official fixed classes = **360 lineage–class pairings**. Each names **one of the 18 existing advanced specializations** (the one its synergy technique adapts) and a class-specific lineage theme. No pairing is a recommendation. This does not introduce 360 new classes, class specializations, automatically granted traits, or additional character power.

### Rules and interpretation

1. **Race and lineage never lock a class:** All 60 lineages may select any of Vanguard, Wildstrider, Shadowblade, Luminary, Arcanist, or Resonant.
2. **Specializations also remain open:** A lineage may choose **any of the three advanced specializations of its class**, with equal standing. The listed specialization is the one its synergy technique adapts; it is not a recommendation, prerequisite, bonus, restricted build or required route.
3. **Themes are design hooks, not new granted powers:** The phrase after the dash describes how that lineage might express the listed specialization. It does **not** add free actions, bonuses, damage, spell access, companions, or skills. Any future mechanical variant must be costed, capped and tested before use.
4. **Core progression is unchanged:** Four free starting class abilities; 12 universal class skills per class; specialization eligibility proposed after five SP in universal skills and a Guild trial; one active specialization; specialization trait plus four sequential advanced skills. Guild ranks F–SS remain independent of character skill progression.
5. **Future racial skill trees are independent:** A lineage has its own innate/learned identity and future racial abilities regardless of chosen specialization. Cross-system interactions can be designed as balanced *sidegrades* rather than cumulative free buffs.
6. **World consistency:** Human regional heritages remain learned traditions; Drakari scale color is cosmetic; Sylph and Skyborn wings are limited to approved gliding/wing-assisted movement; Tidekin have land-use options; Forgeborn do not gain unstated immunities or self-repair.

### Suggested character-creator behavior

1. Select base race and one approved lineage.
2. Select one of six official starting classes, without lineage restrictions.
3. As the player approaches specialization eligibility, show all three of their class specializations as equal choices. Do not mark any as suggested.
4. Where helpful, show the lineage theme as a roleplay or visual-development prompt only. Do not apply a numerical benefit or unlock a power from this table.
5. After the Guild trial, allow the player to choose **any** specialization for the class. Apply only the documented discipline trait and purchased specialization skills.
6. When the future racial skill tree is implemented, show its skills in a separate tab. Review prospective lineage-specialization variants as explicit, versioned mechanical sidegrades requiring the relevant skill, not as free hidden bonuses.

### Future balance and implementation requirements

- **No extra power from a pairing alone.** A highly thematic choice is not more effective than the alternatives.
- **Avoid a 360-skill inflation problem.** Prefer named visual variants, situational prompts, or clearly priced sidegrades to every pairing having a free additional signature ability.
- **Preserve cross-terrain viability.** Aquatic, aerial, underground, heat/cold, and woodland hooks must not prevent general adventure gameplay outside those terrains.
- **Avoid stereotype locks.** Background, race, and lineage cannot dictate morality, personality, religious affiliation, or class choice.
- **Respect action economy.** Beasts, echoes, runes, traps, and coordinated attacks do not produce unlimited extra actions.
- **Tag mechanical revisions.** If a future lineage synergy affects dice, range, duration, healing, movement, or resources, store it as a separately reviewed ability/version with prerequisites and cross-GM text.

## 1. Purpose and terminology

This compendium establishes the **lineage/ancestry choices** for each of Everbound's 12 playable base races. A lineage is a recognizable ancestral variety, body/material type, or (for humans) regional heritage—not a class, subclass, or skill-tree name. Each lineage has a visual or cultural identity and a **core natural specialty** to guide later racial skill design.

- **Race:** What kind of being a character is.
- **Lineage:** The ancestry, biological variety, constructed body type, or heritage within that race.
- **Class:** The character's trained role. Everbound's six official classes are **Vanguard, Wildstrider, Shadowblade, Luminary, Arcanist, and Resonant**.
- **Class specialization:** A distinct advanced playstyle within the selected class.
- **Racial skill tree:** Future skills developed from the selected lineage's innate traits and adapted strengths.

**Character-building rule:** Any race and lineage may choose any of the six official classes. A lineage specialty describes an area of aptitude, **not an automatic granted power or class restriction**. Numerical bonuses, unlock levels, active abilities, limitations, and capstones will be defined in the subsequent racial skill-tree design phase.

**Art consistency:** Preserve Everbound's approved **Painterly JRPG** character direction and base-race anatomy. Lineage variation should remain recognizable within the base race; skin, hair, feather, eye, and scale colors are not automatically gameplay powers.

## 15. Implementation rules for the character creator

1. **Race first:** Player selects one of 12 base races.
2. **Lineage second:** Player selects one of exactly five lineages for that race.
3. **Appearance:** Player customizes appearance within approved race/lineage anatomy (including scale/feather/material colors where appropriate).
4. **Class:** Player selects one of the six official, fixed classes: Vanguard, Wildstrider, Shadowblade, Luminary, Arcanist, Resonant.
5. **Class specialization:** Class advancement follows the existing class-specialization rules.
6. **Racial skill tree:** A separate progression tree tied to the selected lineage, added in a future design phase.
7. **No restrictions by lineage:** All 60 lineages can play all six classes; no custom classes or lineage-locked classes.
8. **Cross-table consistency:** The same character lineage and physical traits persist across Everbound sessions and online character profiles.

# Part III — 60 proposed racial skill trees

# EVERBOUND — Racial & Lineage Skill Trees

**Version:** 1.1 — proposed playtest design · **Date:** November 23, 2026  
**Source of approved roster:** Everbound Racial Lineage Compendium v1.0  
**Companion documents:** Everbound Class & Specialization Compendium v1.1; Lineage × Class Specialization Affinity Atlas v1.1

> **Status:** The 12 races and their 60 exact lineage names are approved. The skills and costs below are **new proposed mechanics**, not previously approved rules. Nothing here replaces the six class foundations, existing class skill trees, or class specializations.

## Rules and separation of powers

- **Choose freely:** Select one of 12 base races, its five lineages, and any of the six fixed classes. No lineage dictates class, morality, personality, religion, or specialization.
- **Innate / learned trait:** Each lineage has one **proposed** free starting trait. For Human heritages it represents training, culture, and upbringing rather than inherited biological superiority. A lineage specialty in the approved roster did **not** previously grant an automatic power; activating these traits requires adoption of this proposal.
- **Two racial branches:** Each lineage has two independent branches, each with Tier I, II and III unlocks. They are **not** new class specializations.
- **Racial Points (RP):** Suggested separate currency from class Skill Points (SP). Spend 1 RP on each racial skill, including the capstone. RP award cadence is **not yet fixed**; award through character progression, never according to whether a player happens to visit a favored biome.
- **Prerequisites:** Unlock Tier I freely with 1 RP; Tier II needs that branch's Tier I, Tier III needs Tier II. A capstone requires a Tier III in either branch and **four racial skills bought** in total.
- **Scale:** Tier I is a minor option; Tier II is a conditional improvement; Tier III is a strong situational action (normally once per encounter if combat-relevant); capstone is once per encounter or a comparable GM-defined major expenditure. No additional combat actions or infinite uses.
- **Action economy:** Skills consume the normal action, movement or reaction appropriate to the effect. The final combat rules will specify which. A successful description does not imply an automatic success when a meaningful hazard/defense calls for a roll.
- **Terrain and accessibility:** Terrain-themed skills have meaningful land, city, ruin, and dungeon applications. No free unrestricted flight, underwater superiority on dry land, instant-kill attacks, immunities, self-repair, or bypassing all locks/wards.
- **Cross-GM:** The same character's RP purchases, capstone uses, and resource state persist across sessions. Guild rank (F–SS) remains separate.

## How to read a skill (new in v1.1)

Every trait, skill and capstone has two lines that players see:

- **Effect:** what the skill lets you do, written positively.
- **Limits:** its boundaries and cost. Specific limits come first, then the standard limits for its tier.

GM notes hold only how to adjudicate. Standard limits by tier: **Trait** — free, always on, gives a clue, never an automatic success. **Tier I** — 1 RP, minor effect. **Tier II** — 1 RP, needs that branch's Tier I, only in the stated situation. **Tier III** — 1 RP, needs Tier II, once per encounter in combat. **Capstone** — 1 RP, needs 4 racial skills including a Tier III, once per encounter.

**Renamed in v1.1:** Crystalforged Tier II *Resonant Brace* → **Lattice Brace** (Cicada Sylph keeps *Resonant Brace*). Frost Orc capstone *Frostmarch* → **Icefang Trek** (Frostmarch stays a region name).

## Implementation notes

1. Store each skill as a versioned `lineage_skill_id`, tier, branch, description, RP cost, prerequisites, use category, and remaining uses. Store free traits separately.
2. Do not require players to prove an ancestral stereotype, disclose biological identity, or roleplay a particular personality to use their lineage's existing skills.
3. Physical identities stay as approved: Beastkin humanoid with one species-appropriate tail; Drakari no automatic wings/breath; Sylph insect wings and glide rather than full flight; Tidekin bipedal and land-viable; Skyborn avian wings and limited wing-assisted movement; Forgeborn visible core gem and constructed body but no unapproved healing/immunity rule.
4. Record exact source/version for future rules changes so cross-GM adjudication is consistent.
5. Numerical bonuses, character-level gates, check DCs, RP cadence, duration and ranges require a separate combat-system playtest. All wording above is **proposed** until approved.

# Part IV — 360 proposed synergy techniques

# EVERBOUND — Lineage × Specialization Synergy Techniques

**Version:** 1.1 — proposed sidegrade playtest design · **Date:** November 23, 2026
**Sources:** Approved Racial Lineage Compendium v1.0, Class & Specialization Compendium v1.1, Lineage × Class Affinity Atlas v1.1.
**Companion:** *EVERBOUND — Racial & Lineage Skill Trees v1.1*

> **Scope:** One named **optional sidegrade** for each of the **360 lineage–class pairings**. Each technique adapts the Tier I skill of one specialization of that class. That pairing is **not a recommendation**: every lineage keeps equal access to all three specializations. These are not new specializations, not automatic racial bonuses and not exclusive powers.

**Changed in v1.1:** recommended specializations removed; each technique's exchange is split into a player-facing **Effect** and **Limits**; Crystalforged prerequisite *Resonant Brace* is now **Lattice Brace**. Technique names are kept as lineage flavour: techniques on the same specialization share one effect.

## Uniform synergy rule (applies to every technique below)

1. **Eligibility:** Own the named lineage, play the indicated class and specialization, and have that specialization's Tier I skill and the named racial Tier II skill.
2. **Cost:** Gain **one synergy training slot** by completing an advanced Guild training objective. It grants no extra combat action or daily pool. Fill or retrain the slot only between adventures. Guild objectives and RP/SP awards must not depend on biome.
3. **One active technique:** Equip at most one. It is an **alternative mode of the named Tier I specialization skill**, not a second activation. Normal actions, resources, range, checks, targets and uses still apply.
4. **Exchange, never stack:** The Limits line says what the original Tier I skill gives up. No extra damage, healing, reactions, companion turns, runes, movement, targets, flight, immunity or free spells.
5. **Other specializations:** A player on one of the class's other two specializations can take the identical-cost **Open Discipline Adaptation**: once per use of that specialization's Tier I skill, replace its combat advantage with a small investigation or navigation cue tied to one of the player's racial Tier II skills. No bonus attack or movement; same action and uses. Themed techniques for those pairings may be written later.
6. **Effect size:** Effects are qualitative until dice, ranges and checks are approved. A GM may allow a small relevant clue or equivalent positional alternative, with no guaranteed success.
7. **World safety:** No instant kills, forced player actions, compulsory spirit truth, unrestricted flight, teleportation or movement, unlimited traps or companions, or material-based poison or healing immunities.

**How to read:** Class → specialization adapted · Technique · Racial prerequisite · Theme · Effect · Limits. Every technique also carries the standard limit: *Needs one synergy training slot · one active technique · no extra actions or resources*.

## Suggested website and digital character-record behavior

- Show the three specialization cards for the player's class as equal choices; never mark one as recommended.
- When specialization Tier I and a relevant Tier II lineage skill are present, display **Synergy training available**. Do not automatically unlock the technique.
- Track one active technique ID, source specialization/skill, source racial skill, training completion, and cooldown/use state inherited from the adapted skill.
- If switching to a different specialization, deactivate the prior variant. Offer the Open Discipline Adaptation option or an appropriately reviewed new synergy; do not transfer a variant's effects to unrelated skills.
- Record the lineage skill and its changed function in the combat log; GMs should see both the original effect **forfeited** and the alternate effect used.

### Example: Wolfkin Wildstrider → Beastwarden

A Wolfkin who owns the racial Tier II skill **Scent Ledger**, the Beastwarden Tier I skill **Coordinated Pounce**, and the synergy-training slot may equip **Packtrail Bond**. On a valid companion command, they can have the companion investigate or signal a relevant scent trail **instead of** creating Coordinated Pounce's attack opening. The companion does not act twice. This adaptation is useful for tracking but does not give a free combat buff.

## Implementation and coverage verification

- **60** approved lineages × **6** fixed classes = **360** named proposed synergy sidegrades, one for each lineage–class pairing.
- Every technique references the correct Tier I ability of the specialization it adapts and a newly designed Tier II lineage skill.
- All 18 existing specializations are used; none are replaced or newly multiplied.
- Numerical modifiers, checks and durations are deliberately reserved for the shared rules-engine design phase.
- **Decided (v1.1):** keep all 360 names as lineage flavour; techniques on the same specialization share one effect.
